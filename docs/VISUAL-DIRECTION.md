# Visual direction and image curation

## The signature (four elements, used with restraint)

1. **The drawing sheet.** The brand kit says its patterns are "taken from how the work is drawn".
   Sections meet on hairlines with registration crosses at the page margins; project facts sit in a
   title block like an architectural sheet; the hero carries the dashed construction lines from the
   logo drawing.
2. **The aperture.** The circle of the mark is a window onto the work. The homepage opens on a
   circular crop of Rembayung's glazed gable that widens to full screen as you scroll ("the moment an
   empty space becomes one that is lived in", kit p.3). Project index rows reveal their photograph in
   a circle; the walkthrough opens each photograph out of the last through the same circle.
3. **The curve as a rule.** The curve is lifted out of the mark and drawn as a hairline divider. It
   keeps its direction: high on the left, settling to the right. Never flipped.
4. **The title pair.** Outfit Medium headline over an EB Garamond italic line in Rikyū Grey, as on
   every page of the kit. Project titles are always Garamond italic.

Buttons are the kit's pills with the open ring (the mark) at the end, and are used only for primary
actions. Everything else is a text link with a hairline.

## Colour and type, as built

Four colours: Chinese Black #141414, Milky White #F3F3F3, Mouse Grey #949495, Rikyū Grey #888E7E
(with the kit's gradient end #404732). Tints of black are used for small text on Milky White, where
Mouse Grey would fail contrast. Rikyū Grey appears as one panel per page at most (the enquiry panel)
and as accents. Outfit 400/500 only; EB Garamond italic 400 only.

## Motion

Purposeful and quiet: images open upward from the horizon as they enter (clip reveal); the hero
aperture and the walkthrough are driven by the visitor's own scrolling; page changes crossfade and
project images morph between the index and the case study (View Transitions). With reduced motion
everything is static, and nothing depends on animation to be read.

## Photography: what was chosen and why

The studio's Drive held about 400 images across five projects. The selection follows the kit's three
kinds of picture, **Space** (a room with light moving through it), **Material** (timber, stone,
plaster and the joints) and **Whole** (the finished space, straight on), and builds a flow across the
site, not just good single frames:

- **Rembayung leads.** It is the best-known project and its photos carry the brand's warmth: timber,
  rattan, batik and daylight. The hero is the glazed gable with the Petronas Twin Towers in the glass,
  which places the project in Kuala Lumpur at a glance. 14 frames were chosen from about 88 usable
  ones. Construction and installation shots (people on site, wrapped furniture) were left out.
- **Ampang balances it.** Professional photography of a white house drawn in black and brass: crisp
  where Rembayung is warm. Frames with the house number and street name were left out for the owners'
  privacy.
- **Proposals sit after built work.** Seri Wirani and Kota Warisan are renders from September 2026
  design presentations. They are labelled "Design visualisation" everywhere and listed after finished
  projects. Their palettes (textured white and timber; white, arches and black frames) echo the built
  work, so the site reads as one body of thinking.
- **Warung Soho** renders are low resolution (1080 px) and more saturated than the brand, so they are
  used small and last until better files arrive.

One gentle grade is applied to every photograph (slightly warm, calmer saturation, softened contrast,
as the kit asks: "grade slightly warm"), and a lighter version to renders, so phone photos, a
professional shoot and renders sit together. No photograph has been altered beyond grading,
resizing and cropping, and all metadata (including GPS on the iPhone originals) is stripped.

## On AI-generated video

Not used. A generated "cinematic" interior would show details that do not exist in the built room,
which would misrepresent the project, and it would mean uploading the client's photographs to a
third-party generator. The motion on the site comes from the real photographs instead. The best
motion asset available is real: the edited film `ROUANG_1.mp4` in the Ampang videographer folder,
plus the Rembayung clips. Both are over the Drive connector's 10 MB limit; once downloaded they can
be cut into short silent loops for the `film` slot that every project supports.
