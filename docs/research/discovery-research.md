Gathered by research agents with web search, then each factual claim was re-checked against its source by a separate fact-checking agent. Verdicts are listed under each topic. Treat "overstated" and "unsupported" claims as unusable.


## rembayung

Rembayung is food creator Khairul Aming's (Khairul Amin Kamarulzaman) first restaurant. It is at Lot 2791, Jalan Daud, Off Jalan Raja Muda Abdul Aziz, Kampung Baru, 50300 Kuala Lumpur, and serves traditional Malay "kampung" food with kuih. It is reservation-only. The plan was announced on 9 Dec 2025 and it opened on 6 Jan 2026. The building is new: two storeys on vacant land reported as 8,000 sq ft, with a reported total investment of RM4 million, built over about a year starting early 2025. Press describes a "semi glass house" or semi-greenhouse building with warehouse-like touches. Inside, it mentions wood textures, greenery and a giant fan, meant to feel like a kampung in the city. Reported capacity is about 250, though Khairul Aming himself has said 200 at a time. The operating company is Rembayung Sdn. Bhd. (1592096-U / 202401046250), which is named in the restaurant's own Instagram bio and in a CTOS listing. No public source I could open credits an interior designer, architect, main contractor or builder. ROUANG / Rouang does not appear anywhere public in connection with Rembayung, and I found no public web presence for a studio of that name at all. So nothing public supports or contradicts ROUANG's interior-design-only claim. Press does credit the concept to Khairul Aming, working with an unnamed "seasoned industry partner": a married couple with restaurant experience, three partners in total. No design or architecture publication feature was found, only news, lifestyle and fan coverage. As of the latest item found (21 Oct 2026), it is still operating as a single outlet in Kampung Baru. No relocation, closure or second branch was found. Several major outlets (Malay Mail direct, Berita Harian, Business Today, The Sun, D&B) blocked fetching, so some coverage could not be checked.

### Recommendations

- Before ROUANG publishes Rembayung as a portfolio project, ask it for documents: a signed appointment or contract with Rembayung Sdn. Bhd. (1592096-U), interior drawings, invoices. Also get written permission from Rembayung Sdn. Bhd. or Khairul Aming to name the project. No public credit exists to fall back on.
- Word any claim strictly as interior design. Public coverage credits the overall concept to Khairul Aming and his unnamed partners: the new two-storey building, built from scratch, and the 'semi glass house' and warehouse-like form. Captions should not suggest ROUANG designed the building or its glasshouse architecture.
- Match ROUANG's case study to what the press says was in the interior: wood textures, greenery, the giant fan, the 'elevated kampung' feel, long and square tables. Ask ROUANG to confirm which of these it designed.
- To find out who did the architecture and construction, check the original build video on Khairul Aming's TikTok, Instagram or X (Dec 2025) for on-screen credits. A formal source would be the building-plan approval with Kuala Lumpur City Hall (DBKL), which would normally name the submitting architect. Only cite either once actually seen.
- Don't quote the conflicting numbers (capacity 200 vs 250, kitchen RM500k vs RM10m, opening 6 vs 7 Jan) without naming the source. Use 6 Jan 2026 and 'about 250 seats' only with attribution.
- Don't use the third-party Threads post about shareholders or directors as a source. It is unverified and names private individuals.

## regulatory

I researched the four topics against official texts where I could reach them. These were the Act 709 reprint as at 1 July 2023, Act 117 as amended to February 2015 (from LAM), Act 520 (from CIDB), Act 599 and Act 730 (from KPDN), and P.U.(A) 449/2024 (from the KPDN repository). I could not reach the Personal Data Protection Department's site (pdp.gov.my) or the Attorney General's Chambers legislation site (lom.agc.gov.my) from this environment: DNS failures and an "Invalid request" error. So the 2024 amendment dates and the 2025 DPO and breach-notification guideline details come from several law-firm summaries that agree with each other, not from the regulator's own pages.

1) PDPA privacy notice. Section 7 of Act 709 lists eight things a written notice must cover. It must be given when the person is first asked for their data, and it must be in both the national language and English (s.7(3)). A Bahasa Malaysia and English privacy notice next to the enquiry form is therefore a legal requirement, not a nice-to-have. The 2024 amendments came in three phases (1 Jan, 1 Apr and 1 Jun 2025):
- "data user" became "data controller"
- data processors are now directly bound by the Security Principle
- maximum penalties for breaking the principles rose to RM1m and/or 3 years
- from 1 June 2025, DPO appointment (s.12A), breach notification (s.12B) and data portability (s.43A) apply
The DPO duty only applies above certain thresholds (more than 20,000 people, more than 10,000 for sensitive data, or regular and systematic monitoring). A small studio probably falls below them.

2) Interior designer title. Under s.27A of the Architects Act 1967, a person who is not registered with LAM as an Interior Designer may not:
- set up an interior design consultancy practice
- describe themselves with "Interior Designer" or an equivalent in any language
- use signs or cards that imply they are one
- sue to recover fees
A firm can only be an interior design consultancy practice if it is registered under s.27E and its owners or directors are registered. Contractors are separate: anyone who carries out, offers to do, or holds themselves out as doing construction works, which includes renovation and alteration, must register with CIDB (Act 520 s.25). The fine is RM10,000 to RM100,000 (s.29).

3) Claims on the website. Both the Consumer Protection Act 1999 (Part II, s.25 penalties) and the Trade Descriptions Act 2011 (ss.17 to 19 and 21) forbid false or misleading claims about services, including claimed qualifications, approvals and affiliations. The advertiser is presumed responsible.

4) Cookie banners. No Malaysian law or regulator guidance I found specifically requires a cookie banner. The Act does not mention cookies. Google's own terms do require you to disclose GA4 and cookies in your privacy policy. Google's consent policy applies only to visitors from the EEA, UK and Switzerland.

### Recommendations

- Publish the privacy notice in both Bahasa Malaysia and English (s.7(3)). Link it right next to the enquiry form's submit button, so it is given when data is first asked for (s.7(2)). Offer the BM version as a language toggle.
- Cover each s.7(1) item in the notice:
- what data the form collects (e.g. name, phone, email, property address, budget, photos)
- the purposes (replying to enquiries, quotations, project delivery, and marketing only if opted in)
- the source (directly from the person)
- access and correction rights, plus a contact email and address
- the classes of third parties (web host, form/CRM/email providers, Google Analytics, contractors or suppliers if a project goes ahead)
- how to limit processing or opt out of marketing
- which fields are required and what happens if they are left blank
Also add a retention period (s.10) and a security statement (s.9).
- Add an unticked consent checkbox for marketing that is separate from the enquiry itself, and keep a record of consents. The direct-marketing objection right (s.43) and the vendor guidance support this. The legal basis for recording consent (Regulation 3) is unverified.
- Use the post-2024 terms in the policy: 'data controller' and 'data processor'. Name the processor classes. Briefly describe breach handling: notifying the Commissioner within 72 hours and affected people within 7 days where the thresholds are met. Treat 'we will appoint a DPO' as needed only if the studio crosses the 20,000 / 10,000 / systematic-monitoring thresholds.
- Mention cross-border transfers (e.g. Google servers abroad). Section 129 was amended from 1 April 2025 to replace the whitelist. Phrase it generally rather than naming specific countries.
- LAM title check: confirm the studio's or its principal's LAM registration in LAM's Registration Check before using 'Interior Designer' or 'Pereka Dalaman'. If registered, display the LAM registration number and practice name as approved by LAM. If not registered, avoid 'Interior Designer(s)' or equivalents in any language on the site, logo, signage and cards. Get local legal advice on wording such as 'interior design studio'.
- CIDB check: if the studio carries out or offers renovation or fit-out works itself, it must be CIDB-registered (Act 520 s.25), and the site can show its CIDB grade and registration. If it is design-only, the copy should say works are carried out by CIDB-registered contractors and must not present the studio itself as the contractor.
- Claims: back up superlatives ('best', 'No.1', 'award-winning'), project photos, client testimonials, years of experience and any affiliation (LAM, MIID, CIDB) with evidence. The CPA s.10(1)(d)/(i) and TDA s.17/18 penalties apply to false or misleading claims, and the advertiser must prove them true under TDA s.18(2). Only show logos of bodies the studio is actually registered with.
- Business disclosures: show the legal entity name, SSM registration number, address, phone and email, and describe services and pricing honestly. This matches the Schedule in the 2024 e-trade regulations if they apply, and builds trust either way.
