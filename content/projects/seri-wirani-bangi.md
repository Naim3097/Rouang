concept: |
  The proposal keeps the house's familiar pitched roof and changes it selectively. Flat horizontal canopies, framed openings and screens are added in chosen places, and old and new are tied together by one consistent datum line.

  Textured white is the background. Timber-toned screens and frames, glass block and planting give the house its identity, so that form, shadow and texture carry the design rather than ornament.
planning: |
  The side garden is treated as an extension of the interior: a usable courtyard rather than leftover space. Thresholds run in sequence, from porch to corridor to garden to interior, so each move into the house is gradual.

  A single visual line runs from the living room, across the garden, to the kitchen, and a translucent glass-block screen separates dining from circulation without closing either off.
materials:
  - name: Textured white
    note: The primary background inside and out, so shadow and texture do the work.
  - name: Timber tone
    note: Screens, frames and joinery that give warmth to a light shell.
  - name: Glass block
    note: Filtered daylight and privacy, on the street front and between shared rooms.
  - name: Dark metal
    note: Fine contrast at fittings, metalwork and lighting.
lighting: |
  Glass block filters daylight while keeping privacy, and full-height openings bring light deep into the plan. In the evening, indirect and concealed linear lighting follows the horizontal lines of the design, with warm task lighting over the kitchen.
challenges:
  - constraint: A street-facing front that needed privacy without closing out daylight.
    response: A recessed glass-block feature framed in warm timber, which lets filtered light in and gives the facade one calm focal point.
  - constraint: Extending the house without losing the character of the original.
    response: New canopies, openings and screens added only at selected points and tied to the existing house by a consistent datum, so the pitched roof stays legible.
  - constraint: Heat and strong sun.
    response: Deep overhangs, slatted screens and planted edges, a tropical layer that shades the walls and softens the mass of the building.
